# Long-lived Tempo MPP channels

The plugin uses the native TIP-1034 reserve at `0x4d50500000000000000000000000000000000000` on the configured Tempo network: Moderato testnet (chain 42431, default) or mainnet (chain 4217). Its integrated wallet signs native type `0x76` transactions and pays gas in that network's OUSD. The peer exchange uses authenticated `pay.stablecoin.v1` messages with protocol version `1`; it does not implement the HTTP MPP challenge/credential envelope.

## Channels, vouchers and bills

Each buyer and seller pair shares one channel, reused by every paid request until it closes. Its terms bind the buyer and seller peers, payer and payee wallets, chain, token, and a random nonce chosen by the buyer, so the pair can open a fresh channel after a close. Their versioned hash is the native channel salt. All amounts are millionths of the selected network's OUSD.

1. **Quote.** The buyer mints an ID for each paid request. Its `Hello` commits to the exact model, the canonical request digest, its byte length, and the output token limit. The seller quotes its rates and cache discount, its view of the channel (the best voucher it holds, `cum_before`, and the sum of its bills, `cost_before`), and an explicit `upfront`: the voucher increase over `cum_before` it requires before work. This seller asks the model's admission deposit, else the node-wide one, when it has no channel with this buyer and the buyer has not paid it since the seller started, and 0 otherwise; the wire allows any amount, so a later prepay mode needs no protocol change. A seller with a closing channel for this buyer refuses the quote. The seller answers `Busy` instead while it has no channel and another request from the buyer is opening one, while a request with the same body is in flight, or while the buyer has three unpaid bills; the buyer says hello again every 250 milliseconds within its admission wait. A request with unknown usage blocks none of these.
2. **Buyer checks.** The buyer pays only when `policy.mode` is `automatic` and `buy_peers` is empty or lists the seller; otherwise it sends the request free. When `buy_prices` has a ceiling for the model, the quoted rates must not exceed it and must give at least its cache discount, and the seller's unbilled credit after funding, the voucher minus the bills the buyer accepted, must stay within the buyer's `admission_deposit`. A new channel can therefore get a full upfront, while later upfronts can only refill credit to that limit. The quote must show the buyer's channel and payee, a `cost_before` no higher than the bills the buyer accepted plus what its other requests may still be billed (lower is fine, since a bill may be on its way or may have overtaken the quote), and a `cum_before` at or below the buyer's own voucher; a seller behind on vouchers then receives the buyer's latest. A quote without a channel is also accepted while the buyer's channel has no bills: the seller never received its first voucher, so the buyer sends that channel. When the views differ otherwise, the buyer refuses the request and recovers its channel. Before opening, topping up or signing anything, the buyer prices the worst case: every byte of the canonical body as an input token plus the full output limit, at the quoted rates. The upfront plus this request's share of the voucher increase must fit `cap`, when set, and the voucher increases must fit the remaining daily budget. The voucher that bill would need must fit the deposit plus one top-up. The actual bill is checked again in step 6.
3. **Funding.** Without a channel, the buyer journals a signed native `open` with a deposit of `channel_topup`, and waits up to 25 seconds for it to finalize. Once the channel is open it signs `Voucher(bytes32 channelId,uint96 cumulativeAmount)` for the seller's `cum_before` plus the upfront, or reuses its existing voucher when that is already higher, and persists it, with the day's spending, before sending it. An open or top-up still pending after the wait is a rare fallback: the buyer refuses the request with a 402 and sends `Cancel`, so the request does not keep holding the seller's slot for its body, and the open continues in the background. The EIP-712 domain is `TIP20 Channel Reserve`, version `1`, the configured chain ID, and the canonical reserve address. Channel IDs also include the chain ID, so vouchers cannot be replayed across networks. Only primitive secp256k1 signatures are supported.
4. **Admission.** The seller's wallet verifies the opening event, the voucher signature, and that the finalized and latest deposit back the voucher, with nothing settled and no close requested. The voucher must be at least `cum_before` plus the upfront; the buyer's resent or reused vouchers always are. The voucher the seller already stores, which an existing channel presents when the upfront is 0, is admitted without these chain reads: its signature and opening cannot change, the deposit only grows, and a close request moves the channel to closing on the next watch tick, within the close deferral. The seller keeps the best voucher and answers `Ready`; the buyer then dispatches once. An admitted voucher that the seller verified and that is above zero gives the buyer standing, so its next channel opens with no upfront and a zero voucher.
5. **Bill.** Terminal usage fixes the bill: `ceil((uncached_input × input_rate + cached_input × input_rate × (100 - discount)/100 + min(output, output_limit) × output_rate) / 1,000,000)`. Arithmetic saturates rather than wraps. The seller caps the bill at the worst case checked before dispatch, absorbing any prompt longer than the body bound, and sends `Bill{seq, usage, cost, running_total}`, where `seq` numbers the channel's bills from 0. Usage that arrives after the channel began closing is never billed, since the capture is already fixed.
6. **Payment.** The buyer records the usage it observed on its own terminal event. The bill may arrive before or after it, and bills may arrive out of order: the buyer accepts the bill numbered next and holds a later one until then. The buyer signs a voucher up to the running total only when the billed usage equals its own, the cost matches the quoted rates capped at the worst case, the running total equals the bills it accepted plus this cost, the cost is at most the worst case checked before dispatch, and the upfront plus the voucher increase fits `cap`. The daily budget is not checked again: as in the Lightning plugin, the bills of dispatched work are paid even when concurrent requests overshoot it. The policy mode does not apply here: work dispatched before a switch to `free_only` is still paid. Otherwise it refuses without signing and records why. Upfront credit pays bills first, so a covered bill needs no new voucher. When a voucher would exceed the deposit, the buyer tops up once by `channel_topup`, and later bills wait for it; the check before dispatch ensures that one top-up is enough for each bill. The top-up must finalize within the seller's 120-second payment time.
7. **Close.** The seller captures `min(best voucher, billed total)` with `close(descriptor, cum, capture, voucher)`; the rest of the deposit returns to the buyer atomically. A capture of 0 needs no voucher. The plugin never calls `settle`, so nothing moves before the close.

The seller closes a channel:

- when a bill stays unpaid 120 seconds after it was first sent;
- after one hour without requests;
- when it sees the buyer's close request. A running request may still be billed first, but the seller closes within 600 seconds, inside the reserve's 900-second grace.

The buyer requests close on a channel it has not used for two hours and withdraws the whole deposit after the grace, since nothing settles before a close. If a top-up cancels a pending close request, recovery requests close again. The seller repeats its close notice on the buyer's next request until the buyer has recorded the close; the buyer verifies every close notice on chain.

Daily spending counts the voucher increases the buyer signs, not channel deposits. Opens and top-ups are capital that returns at close. A `free_only` policy sends new requests free before anything is signed or opened; it does not stop the bills of work already dispatched.

## Trust

The buyer's voucher authorizes the bills it acknowledged plus any unused upfront credit, and chain enforcement only bounds the capture by the voucher. The unbilled credit never exceeds the buyer's `admission_deposit`, however often a seller asks for an upfront. A seller can therefore keep that credit for a bill the buyer refused or for overstated usage, but it cannot capture a larger bill the buyer did not sign. The buyer records an error when a close captures more than its acknowledged bills. Vouchers do not prove computation, so choose sellers and deposits accordingly.

## Configuration and limits

One [combined JSON configuration](../README.md#install-and-configure) contains the engine policy and nested wallet settings. There is one installed plugin and no wallet binding. In `automatic` mode, `channel_topup` (1 OUSD unless set) must not exceed `wallet.max_payment` and must be at least `cap`, or at least the daily budget without a cap. Startup writes `policy`, `prices`, and a set `admission_deposit` into the ledger, and controls change them at runtime; every start re-applies the configured values, so runtime changes revert unless the configuration file changes too. Each wallet transaction's fee is bounded by both the caller's limit and `wallet.max_fee`, and `wallet.max_payment` bounds each open or top-up deposit. Both peers need an unlocked, gas-funded wallet.

Clients send no payment fields: buyer policy decides which requests are paid. A paid request needs an exact model, an output limit of 1..4096 in `max_tokens` or `max_completion_tokens`, and a body within 64 KiB. For SSE include `stream_options.include_usage` in the original body. Requests to one seller run at once, in each direction, so a node that also sells to that peer can still buy from it. A new request waits, within the admission wait, only for a request with the same body to release its slot and for the buyer's first request to open the channel. The seller matches a forwarded request to the buyer's agreed request by the authenticated peer and the digest of the body as the client sent it, which must equal the agreed one; that protects the seller's bill cap and the buyer's checks before dispatch. A forwarded request is admitted under its agreement even after the seller removes the price, and runs only once. On buying nodes and authenticated serving ingress, chat-completion routing requires an inspectable body within the same 64 KiB bound even when the selected route is free; a missing or oversized body cannot bypass identity checks.

Each node handles a peer's messages one at a time in the order the plugin receives them.

## Failure and recovery

An authenticated `QuoteRejected` response closes only a buyer request that has no agreed quote. An `Unpriced` response, from a seller that does not price the model, closes it the same way and the buyer sends the request free. A buyer request that was not dispatched when its HTTP request ended, for example after a restart or a host deadline, is cancelled with the seller on the next retry tick; any voucher it signed stays as channel credit. A generic peer error remains retryable, including when a locked seller has already saved a quote whose reply was lost.

A seller that restarts locked withholds `Ready` until it can sign closes. A dispatched request is admitted from the voucher verified at admission and the channel state, without another chain call; a channel moved to closing admits no inference. A buyer request that was dispatched but never billed closes locally one hour after its quote expired, without changing the channel.

The wallet journals the channel terms, nonce, signed bytes, signing context, and transaction hash before broadcast. Uncertain outcomes reuse those bytes. An unresolved nonce blocks another signature. Receipt finality and expiry use one finalized block cutoff. Missing receipts with a consumed nonce keep an attempt pending. After a confirmed revert or proven expiry, top-ups, closes and payer recovery may sign a new attempt for the identical intent, while retaining every prior signed transaction. Opens are never recreated: an open that expired unsent or reverted closes the channel and refuses the request.

The seller persists terminal usage and the bill before acknowledging the terminal hook, and sends the bill in the background. Each channel close commits together with closing its requests. Restart reconciles the original transactions, requests and channels. Interrupted seller execution becomes usage unknown and cannot execute again. A buyer interrupted before it recorded its own usage cannot verify that request's bill and refuses it.

Known usage is billed even if HTTP delivery fails or is cancelled. Missing usage is never turned into a measured zero charge, and it does not block the pair. The seller keeps the request as unknown for the audit trail and sends `Unbilled` for it, repeated on each retry tick and on the buyer's next hello until the buyer acknowledges it with `UnbilledSeen`. The buyer closes its own request unpaid without changing its view of the channel, and its next request is quoted and served on the same channel. From the first notice, or from a later quote, the seller never bills the request, so bills stay in order: late usage is recorded but not billed. Without the notice, the buyer closes the request one hour after its quote expired. `waive` is operator bookkeeping: it only closes a request the buyer was told about, and otherwise sends a zero bill. Unstarted requests may be cancelled; any upfront stays as channel credit.

`wallet.max_settlement_attempts` defaults to three failed signed attempts per top-up, `close`, `request_close` or `withdraw` step, and accepts 1 to 10. Close and recovery attempts expire after one hour; opens and top-ups after 10 minutes. Expired and reverted attempts count toward the limit; a close request later cancelled by a top-up does not. Retry counts survive restarts. Exhaustion remains an explicit error.

The payer may independently start the native grace period at any time. Long inference or an unavailable seller can outlast it. The plugin cannot stop a backend already executing or recover usage the host never delivered. Production needs enforceable execution deadlines and durable terminal observations in Mesh.

```sh
mesh-llm plugins call tempo control --input '{"command":"pending"}'
mesh-llm plugins call tempo control --input '{"command":"retry","request_id":"REQUEST_UUID"}'
mesh-llm plugins call tempo control --input '{"command":"cancel","request_id":"REQUEST_UUID"}'
mesh-llm plugins call tempo control --input '{"command":"recover","request_id":"REQUEST_UUID"}'
mesh-llm plugins call tempo control --input '{"command":"waive","request_id":"REQUEST_UUID"}'
mesh-llm plugins call tempo control --input '{"command":"unblock","peer":"BUYER_NODE_ID"}'
```

`cancel` and `recover` run on the buyer; `recover` closes the channel with a seller, named by `peer` or the only open one, through `requestClose`; the seller then closes it, or the buyer withdraws after the grace. `waive` runs on the seller only for unknown usage. `unblock` runs on the seller and forgives the bills a buyer's closed channels lost, so they no longer count toward its three unpaid bills. `retry` without an ID reconciles pending wallet transactions and every active request and channel. `pending` and `transactions` show requests, channels and wallet transactions without signed bytes or voucher signatures. The full command list is in the [README](../README.md#inspect-and-control).

## Evidence and acceptance

Local tests exercise actual native signing, ABI and event shapes for opens, top-ups, close requests, closes with a cumulative voucher and capture, withdrawals and transfers, vouchers, independent receipt validation, engine-to-wallet calls, crash and restart boundaries, and exact withdrawal timing through loopback fixtures. These fixtures are not a Tempo node. The default host smoke uses fabricated wallet evidence and canned inference. The [earlier native trial](testnet-trial.md) and the [companion-host trial](tempo-exit-testnet-trial.md) passed with the earlier design that opened one reserve per request; the long-lived channels have not yet run on Moderato. Broader failure acceptance and latency benchmarking remain outstanding.
